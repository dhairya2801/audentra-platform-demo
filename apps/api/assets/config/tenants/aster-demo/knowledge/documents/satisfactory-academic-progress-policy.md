## The three standards

Financial aid — federal, state and Aster need- and merit-based — requires
**satisfactory academic progress**, measured on the whole academic record
(including transferred credits) at the end of every Fall and Spring term when
final grades post:

| Standard | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Qualitative** | Cumulative GPA of **2.00** or higher |
| **Pace (quantitative)** | Credits completed ÷ credits attempted of **67%** or higher. Attempted credits include W, I, NP, F and repeated courses; transfer credits count as attempted and completed |
| **Maximum timeframe** | Total attempted credits within **150% of the program's published length** — 180 attempted credits for a 120-credit degree, 183 for 122, 186 for 124, 189 for 126, 192 for 128, 195 for 130 |

New students are *meeting* until their first evaluation; a transfer
student's record is evaluated on the transferred work at admission.

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning | Aid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meeting** | All three standards met | Continues |
| **Warning** | First term below the GPA or pace standard | Continues for one term automatically; no appeal needed |
| **Not meeting** | A second consecutive term below a standard, or the maximum timeframe reached or unreachable | **Aid is suspended** for the next term unless an appeal is approved |
| **Probation** | An appeal was approved | Continues for one term under an academic plan; the plan's terms must be met each term until the student is meeting again |

The status is shown on the Financials page with the figures behind it.

## Appeals

A student who is *not meeting* may appeal within **ten business days** of
the notice (portal form to Financial Aid) explaining the circumstances —
illness, injury, a death in the family, a change in work or family
responsibilities — what has changed, and an **academic plan** agreed with
the adviser that reaches the standards by a stated term. The **SAP Appeals
Committee** meets weekly on Tuesdays and decides within ten business days;
the decision is emailed and recorded on the aid record. A student whose
appeal is denied may attend at their own expense and regain eligibility once
the standards are met.

## Relation to academic standing

Academic standing (Registrar) and SAP (Financial Aid) use the same grades
and the same 2.0 line but differ: standing decides whether a student may
continue; SAP decides whether aid continues. A student can be in good
standing but not meeting SAP (for example because of pace), or on academic
probation but meeting SAP under an approved plan.
