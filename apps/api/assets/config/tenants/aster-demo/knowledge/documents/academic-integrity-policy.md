## What counts as a violation

- **Plagiarism**: presenting another person's words, code, data or ideas as
  one's own without attribution, including self-plagiarism of work
  submitted for another course.
- **Unauthorised collaboration**: working with others on work the
  instructor set as individual.
- **Cheating**: unauthorised materials or devices in an assessment;
  copying; impersonation.
- **Fabrication**: invented data, sources or citations.
- **Unauthorised use of generative tools**: using AI writing or code
  generation where the course syllabus prohibits it, or failing to disclose
  it where disclosure is required. Each syllabus states the course's rule;
  where it is silent, generative tools may be used for brainstorming and
  editing but not to produce submitted text or code, and their use must be
  acknowledged.

## Process

1. The instructor discusses the concern with the student and **reports**
   the case to Student Conduct within ten business days, with the evidence
   and a proposed course-level sanction.
2. Student Conduct checks for prior violations and notifies the student,
   who may accept the finding or contest it at a hearing (see *Code of
   Student Conduct*).
3. **Sanctions** (typical): first violation — zero on the work and the
   online integrity module; second (in any course) — F in the course,
   disciplinary probation and a transcript notation for one year; third —
   suspension for at least one term. Fabrication of research data or
   impersonation may be treated as a second violation at the first
   instance.

A student may not withdraw from a course to avoid an integrity sanction;
a W recorded after a report is reverted.

## Appeals

Within five business days, to the Academic Standards Committee, on
procedural or evidentiary grounds; the grade consequence is decided with
the finding and is not separately appealable under the *Grading policy*.
