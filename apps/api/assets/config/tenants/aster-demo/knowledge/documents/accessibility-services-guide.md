## Who can register

Any student with a documented disability — learning, attention, psychiatric,
chronic health, sensory, mobility, or a temporary condition such as a
concussion or a broken arm. Registration is confidential: instructors and
offices see only the accommodation letter, never the diagnosis.

## How to register

1. Submit the request form on the portal (Profile → Accessibility) with
   **documentation**: for learning and attention disabilities a
   psycho-educational evaluation within the last three years; for other
   conditions a clinician's letter stating the diagnosis, functional
   limitations and recommendations. A secondary-school accommodation plan
   is helpful but not sufficient on its own.
2. Attend an **intake meeting** (Tuesdays and Thursdays; virtual available)
   within ten business days of submitting.
3. Receive the **accommodation letter**, delivered to the student and to
   each instructor through the portal at the start of every term the
   student renews it (renewal is one click on the Accessibility page).

Onboarding asks about accommodation interest; answering "yes" starts the
request but does not by itself register the student.

## Common accommodations

Extended time (typically 1.5×) and a reduced-distraction room for
assessments; note-taking support; accessible course materials; flexibility
on attendance and deadlines where the condition is episodic; priority
registration; a reduced course load that still counts as full time for
housing and scholarships (and for immigration, through a DSO's medical
reduced course load); housing accommodations (see below); release from the
meal-plan requirement on medical grounds.

## Housing and animals

Housing accommodations (a single room, an accessible room, a specific hall,
air conditioning, proximity to a dining hall) should be requested by **22
July 2026** — 30 days before move-in — so they can be built into the
assignment; later requests are met as space allows. Service animals are
permitted anywhere without registration; **emotional support animals** in
housing require approval by Accessibility Services with a clinician's
letter, at least 30 days before the animal arrives.

## Temporary conditions

Injuries and short illnesses are handled without full registration: submit a
clinician's note and receive temporary accommodations for up to one term.

## Disagreements

A student who believes an accommodation was wrongly denied or not provided
may raise it with the Director of Accessibility Services and then with the
university's ADA coordinator (Larkin Hall 420).
