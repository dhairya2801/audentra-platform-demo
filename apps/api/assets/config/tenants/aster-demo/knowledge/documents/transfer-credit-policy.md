## What transfers

Credit is awarded for courses taken at a regionally or nationally accredited
institution with a grade of **C (2.0) or better**, when the course is
college-level and comparable in content to an Aster course or fits a
general-education area. Grades do not transfer; only credits do, so
transferred work does not affect the Aster GPA.

Limits:

- at most **60 credits** from two-year institutions;
- at most **90 credits** in total from all sources;
- the **last 30 credits** of a degree must be earned at Aster (residency
  requirement), and at least half of the major's upper-level credits;
- remedial, vocational and physical-education activity courses do not
  transfer;
- courses older than ten years in computing, nursing and the natural
  sciences are reviewed by the department before credit is awarded.

Examination credit: Advanced Placement scores of 4 or 5, International
Baccalaureate higher-level scores of 5 or above, and CLEP scores at the
American Council on Education recommended level receive credit for the
matching Aster course.

## How evaluation works

1. The **official transcript** arrives (see *Offer acceptance and
   conditional admission*). A student-uploaded copy lets evaluation begin but
   credit is posted only from the official copy.
2. A **transcript evaluator** in the Registrar's office matches each course
   against the equivalency table. Courses with no existing rule go to the
   department for a decision within ten business days.
3. Credit posts to the record within **15 business days** of the official
   transcript's receipt, and the Classrooms page shows each transferred
   course as *exempted* on the academic plan, so the adviser can plan around
   it.

Transfer Orientation (27 August 2026) includes an evaluation review session;
transfer students should bring course descriptions or syllabi for anything
marked *pending department decision*.

## Appeals

A student who disagrees with an evaluation asks the Transfer Credit
Evaluator for a re-review with a syllabus within **30 days** of the credit
posting; unresolved cases go to the department chair, whose decision is
final. A course denied for a low grade cannot be appealed.
