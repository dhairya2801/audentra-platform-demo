## The core

| Area | Credits | Courses that satisfy it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Academic Writing | 3 | WRIT 101 Academic Writing Seminar (first term; placement may require WRIT 099 first) |
| Aster Seminar | 1 | AST 100 Aster Seminar (first term, first-year students only) |
| Quantitative Reasoning | 3–4 | MATH 151 Calculus I, MATH 120 Quantitative Reasoning, or STAT 101 Introduction to Statistics |
| Laboratory Science | 4 | BIO 101, CHEM 101, PHYS 151 or ENVS 110 |
| Humanities | 6 | Two of ENGL 120, ENGL 210, ENGL 220, HIST 110, PHIL 101 |
| Social Science | 6 | Two of ECON 101, ECON 102, PSY 101, SOC 101, POLS 101 |
| Arts | 3 | ART 101, DES 101, DES 120 |
| Global Perspectives | 3 | HIST 110, ENGL 120, or a course marked *global* in the catalog |
| Ethics | 3 | PHIL 220 Ethics, or a program's designated ethics course |
| Free elective within the core | 4 | Any course outside the major |

Courses may count for the major and the core at the same time (a Biology
major's BIO 101 satisfies Laboratory Science), but a single course satisfies
at most two core areas (for example HIST 110 counts as Humanities and
Global Perspectives). Core courses may not be taken pass/no-pass.

## Timing

Advisers plan the core across the first two years: WRIT 101, AST 100 and the
quantitative course in the first term, the laboratory science in the first
year, and the distribution areas around the major's sequence. A first-term
schedule for most first-year students is 14–16 credits: WRIT 101, AST 100,
the quantitative course, one major course and one distribution course.

## Placement

WRIT 101 placement is by the application record; students admitted
conditionally on English take the placement assessment at International
Check-In and may be placed into WRIT 099 Academic English (credits do not
count towards the degree). Mathematics placement (MATH 120, MATH 151 or a
preparatory course) is by the Mathematics placement test taken online
before the advising meeting; students with AP Calculus 4 or 5 receive credit
for MATH 151.

## Transfer and examination credit

Transferred courses satisfy core areas when the Registrar's evaluation maps
them to an Aster course in the area; a general "humanities elective"
transfer satisfies the Humanities area. AP, IB and CLEP credit is applied
the same way. The Aster Seminar cannot be transferred and is waived for
transfer students entering with 24 or more credits.
